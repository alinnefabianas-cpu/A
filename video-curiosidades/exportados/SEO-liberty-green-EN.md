# Description
The Statue of Liberty wasn't always green. It used to be shiny, like a brand-new penny. 🗽

When it was unveiled in 1886, its copper skin (only about as thick as two pennies) was a bright reddish-brown. Standing in the harbor, in rain and salty sea air, the copper slowly reacted with the air and water: dark brown, then dull, then green. By about 1906 it was almost completely green, and officials even planned to paint it. But the green layer, called patina, isn't damage at all: it seals the copper and protects it from the weather.

Did you know she used to be copper-colored? 👇
